Lapel — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 12 October 2026

Lapel is a wardrobe app. This policy describes exactly what it sends, where it goes, and what is kept. It describes the app as it actually behaves, not as it might one day.

There is no account

Lapel does not ask for a name, an email address, or a password, and there is nothing to sign in to. Nothing you do in the app is associated with your identity, because the app never learns it.

What stays on your device

Your wardrobe lives on your phone and nowhere else:

  • every garment, including its photograph
  • outfits you save or wear
  • trips and packing lists
  • your preferences and settings

This is stored in a private database inside the app. It is not synced to Lapel, and not readable by anyone else. Lapel keeps no copy of it, so there is nothing for us to restore.

Backing up to your own iCloud

If backup is switched on — Settings › Backup — your closet and its photographs are copied into your iCloud, in the app's private area, using the iCloud account your iPhone is already signed in to. There is no Lapel account, and nothing goes to Lapel's servers: this is your device talking to Apple, and it is stored against your iCloud storage.

Switch it off and nothing is copied. Deleting the app does not delete the backup; deleting Lapel's data from your iCloud storage settings does.

What is sent, and why

Lapel uses AI for three things. In each case the request goes to Lapel's own server, which forwards it to Anthropic (the provider of the Claude model) and returns the answer.

Identifying a garment from a photo. When you add an item, its photograph is sent to be identified. The photo is held only for the length of that request and is not stored by Lapel.

Suggesting an outfit or packing a trip. A description of the relevant garments is sent — things like type, colour, pattern, warmth and when each was last worn — along with the occasion and the weather. Photographs are not sent for these requests.

The weather. If you set a location, its name and coordinates are sent to Open-Meteo to retrieve a forecast. No other information accompanies this request.

Technical information

A device identifier. A random identifier is generated on your phone the first time you use AI tagging. It is sent with photo requests and when checking your allowance or your subscription, so that what you are entitled to can be counted. It is not derived from your device's hardware and is not linked to you. A one-way hash of it — never the identifier itself — is given to RevenueCat so a subscription can be matched to a device; see Subscriptions below.

Your IP address. Like any internet service, Lapel's server sees the address your request came from. It is used to apply rate limits, is stored only in memory and only briefly, is hashed rather than kept in the clear, and is never written to logs or a database.

Subscriptions

If you subscribe, payment is handled by Apple. Lapel never sees your card, and no payment details reach Lapel's servers.

Lapel uses RevenueCat to confirm what a subscription entitles you to. RevenueCat receives a one-way hash of your device identifier, and the purchase details Apple provides: which plan was bought, when it renews, and whether a trial is running. It receives no name, no email address, no payment details, and nothing about your wardrobe.

What you are entitled to is always decided from the purchase record, never from anything the app claims about itself.

What Lapel does not do

  • No advertising identifier is read or used
  • No analytics or usage tracking is transmitted
  • Nothing is shared with data brokers, advertisers, or any third party beyond those named above
  • Your activity is not tracked across other apps or websites

Crash reports

This one is on when you install Lapel, and you can turn it off at any time in Settings.

When the app fails, a technical report is sent: what went wrong, where in the code, the app version, your iOS or Android version, and your device model. It describes the program, not you. It carries no photographs, no garments, no trips, no location and nothing that identifies you or your device — the app strips those before anything is sent.

It is on by default because a crash nobody reports is a crash nobody fixes, and the people most likely to hit one are the least likely to go hunting for a setting.

Reports are handled by Sentry and stored in the European Union. Turn the switch off and sending stops immediately, including anything already waiting to be sent.

Third parties

These services receive data, and only what is described above:

  • Anthropic — processes AI requests. See anthropic.com/legal/privacy
  • Open-Meteo — returns weather forecasts. See open-meteo.com/en/terms
  • Apple iCloud — holds your backup, if you switch it on, in your own iCloud account. See apple.com/legal/privacy
  • Sentry — receives crash reports, only if you switch them on. Stored in the EU. See sentry.io/privacy
  • RevenueCat — confirms what your subscription entitles you to, if you subscribe. See revenuecat.com/privacy

Lapel's own server does not keep a copy of your photographs or your wardrobe.

Children

Lapel is not directed at children and does not knowingly collect information from anyone.

Beta testing

Lapel is currently distributed to a small number of testers through Apple's TestFlight. Apple provides the developer with information about testers — such as installs, sessions and crash counts — under Apple's own privacy policy.

Changes

If this policy changes, the date at the top will change with it. Material changes will be described in the app.
